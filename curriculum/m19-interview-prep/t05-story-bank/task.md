Build the first three entries of your behavioural story bank. These are real stories from your own work, written so you can tell them in about two minutes each in an interview. Use the STAR structure from lesson 5. Write 900 to 1,500 words in total.

## The prompts

Write one story for each:

1. **"Tell me about a production incident or a bug you caused. What did you change afterwards?"**
2. **"Tell me about a time you shipped with incomplete requirements. How did you decide what to build?"**
3. **"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate or your manager."**

Frontend stories are welcome. Where you can, pick ones with a backend or API angle: a contract you negotiated, a server-side cause you tracked down, an endpoint you wrote.

## For each story, write

- **Situation** and **Task:** two to four sentences together. Team, product, your role, what was at stake.
- **Action:** the bulk of the story. What *you* did, step by step, with concrete details (tools, numbers, what was said, what you decided and why). Use "I" for your part.
- **Result:** what happened, with a number or an observable outcome if you have one, and what you learned or changed.
- **Two likely follow-up questions** an interviewer would ask, with a one- or two-sentence answer to each.
- **Other prompts it covers:** which other common prompts from lesson 5 the same story could answer.

## Then add

A short **story bank table** with the three stories as rows and these columns: story name, prompts covered, the number or outcome, the backend angle.

If you do not have a real story for one prompt, say so and write the closest one you have, explaining what you would emphasise. Do not invent events: an interviewer's follow-ups find invented details quickly, and the point of this exercise is a bank you can actually use.

## What is assessed

Structure (a short setup and a long, specific action), whether the actions are clearly yours, the specificity of details and results, whether each story shows a lesson or change in behaviour, and whether the follow-ups anticipate what an interviewer would really ask. There are no automated checks.
